> Looking for TL;DR? Check [key takeaways](#key-takeaways)

In three different embedding models, the vector is close to useless for search until the last quarter of the layers. Half the final nDCG@10 first shows up at **75% to 100%** depth on 37 of 39 model and dataset pairs (mxbai, MiniLM and Qwen3, on 13 NanoBEIR datasets). The exception is Quora on the two encoders, where early layers already work.

> This is the third post in the embedding geometry series. [Part 1](/blog/embedding-layers) found that mxbai's tokens only converge in its last five layers, on 5 to 20 hand-picked sentences. [Part 2](/blog/similarity-attractor) covers the similarity attractor. This post re-runs those measurements at scale and adds a real search test. [The Jacobian lens post](/blog/jacobian-lens-embeddings) reads what goes into the vector during those last layers. The Matryoshka slice results that used to be here are now in [their own post](/blog/matryoshka-slices).

## Search quality shows up only near the end

I read the vector out of every layer of three different embedding models and searched with it:

| model                                                                             | kind          | layers | pooling (how the vector is taken)  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | -----: | ---------------------------------- |
| [mxbai-embed-large-v1](https://huggingface.co/mixedbread-ai/mxbai-embed-large-v1) | BERT encoder  |     24 | the CLS token, put before the text |
| [all-MiniLM-L6-v2](https://huggingface.co/sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2) | small encoder |      6 | the average of all token vectors   |
| [Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)          | LLM decoder   |     28 | the last token                     |

The data is [NanoBEIR](https://huggingface.co/collections/zeta-alpha-ai/nanobeir-66e1a0af21dfd93e620cd9f6): 13 small versions of the BEIR datasets, 649 queries in all, up to 5,000 documents each.

Take the SciFact claim "Headaches are not correlated with cognitive impairment." Here is its nDCG@10 (from 0 to 100) with each model's vector read out at different depths:

```
depth          0%    50%    75%    90%   100%
mxbai           0      0      0     30    100
MiniLM          0      0      0      0    100
Qwen3           0      0      0    100    100
```

It finds nothing for three quarters of every model, then finds the right abstract at the top. It's one of 83 queries, out of 649, that score 0 through the first half of all three models and 100 at the end.

Over all 649 queries, with each model's own pooling. "No hit" is the share of queries with no relevant document in the top 10:

```
               depth   layer   nDCG@10 p25   p50   mean   no hit
mxbai           0%      L0          0      0     0.45    98.3%
                50%     L12         0      0     1.75    95.8%
                75%     L18         0      0     0.53    96.9%
                92%     L22         0  21.40    33.11    40.4%
               100%     L24     38.69  70.20    66.06     9.7%

MiniLM          0%      L0          0      0    16.15    67.8%
                50%     L3          0      0    12.56    74.4%
                83%     L5          0      0    16.88    66.4%
               100%     L6      30.10  60.83    56.91    14.2%

Qwen3           0%      L0          0      0     0.40    98.5%
                50%     L14         0      0     0.64    97.1%
                75%     L21         0      0    23.14    52.4%
                89%     L25     16.81  50.88    51.29    20.6%
               100%     L28     38.69  63.09    64.91     9.4%
```

Observations:

- mxbai gets its search vector in the last 3 of 24 layers. Its median query scores 0 until layer 22, and half of the final score first shows up at layer 22 or 23 on 12 of the 13 datasets.
- MiniLM gets it in its last layer alone. It reaches half its final score at layer 6 on 12 of 13 datasets. Before that, 66% to 77% of queries find nothing.
- Qwen3 builds it more gradually, over its last quarter. It reaches half the final score between layer 21 and layer 26 on all 13 datasets.
- The exception is Quora for the two encoders. MiniLM already scores 78 at layer 0 there, and mxbai's CLS reaches 73 at layer 8. Quora pairs are near copies of each other, like "What is it like to be an IITian?" and "What does it feel like to be an IITian?". I haven't traced why that's enough for an early layer. Qwen3 scores 0 to 3 on Quora up to layer 17.

This timing comes from how the models are trained. A model trained to work at every layer exit behaves differently: in [2D Matryoshka](/blog/mxbai-2d-matryoshka), mxbai-embed-2d-large-v1 scores r = 0.790 against human similarity at L12, where standard mxbai scores 0.635 in the same test.

## The middle layers search worse than layer 0

At layer 0, the average of the token vectors is a plain bag of context-free words, which is what a [static embedding](/blog/static-embeddings) does. In all three models it beats the middle of the model:

```
mean pooling      layer 0    middle layer    last layer
mxbai              18.57      10.34 (L12)      65.65
MiniLM             16.15      11.65 (L2)       56.91
Qwen3              16.61       3.95 (L12)      25.76
```

"Where was the university at which Barrie Ciliberti was a professor located?" is an example. mxbai scores 61 with its layer 0 average, 0 at layer 12, and 85 with its final CLS vector. MiniLM goes 61, 0 at layer 3, and 61 at the end. (Qwen3's last-layer average is low because Qwen3 was trained to use the last token.)

So you can't save compute by searching with a middle layer. The next two sections show why on sentence pairs. The rest of the post is mxbai only. It uses mean pooling over all 25 layers (the embedding layer plus 24 transformer layers), on 500 [SNLI](https://nlp.stanford.edu/projects/snli/) contradiction pairs and 1,379 [STS-B](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mteb/stsbenchmark-sts) pairs scored by people from 0 (unrelated) to 1 (same meaning).

## The middle layers make every pair look alike

[Part 2](/blog/similarity-attractor) found a "similarity attractor": around layer 12, all sentence pairs move toward high cosine similarity, whatever they mean. SNLI contradictions are the hardest case, because both sentences share the topic and the words:

> Premise: _"Two large dogs running in some grass."_  
> Hypothesis: _"There is no grass near the two dogs."_

On 500 such pairs, the mean cosine peaks at **0.914 at L12**:

<PlotlyChart data={scaleData.snli_attractor.data} layout={scaleData.snli_attractor.layout} />

| Layer | Mean cosine | What's happening                                            |
| ----- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| L0    | 0.653       | Raw token embeddings, modest similarity (shared vocabulary) |
| L6    | 0.723       | Early layers raise the floor for all pairs                  |
| L12   | **0.914**   | Peak attractor, contradictions look nearly identical        |
| L18   | 0.839       | Middle layers start building semantic contrast              |
| L24   | 0.617       | Final layer discriminates, but only by 0.297 from peak      |

The model recovers from 0.914 back to 0.617, only slightly below where it started (0.653). It doesn't make contradictions look _dissimilar_. It makes them look _less identical than they were at L12_.

STS-B shows the attractor hits every kind of pair. Binned by the human score:

<PlotlyChart data={scaleData.stsb_binned.data} layout={scaleData.stsb_binned.layout} />

- All four bins peak near L12. Pairs people rated unrelated (0 to 0.3) peak at 0.924, and near-paraphrases (0.8 to 1.0) at 0.967. [check: the text said these are 0.048 apart, but 0.967 − 0.924 = 0.043]
- At L0, the unrelated and near-paraphrase bins are 0.169 apart (0.702 vs 0.871). By L12 they are only 0.037 apart.
- At L24 they end at 0.570 and 0.930, a 0.360 spread. The last 12 layers rebuild that gap from 0.037.

That's the search result above, seen on pairs. At L12 the right document and the wrong ones look alike, so the vector can't tell them apart.

## Meaning comes back only in the last layers

The Pearson correlation between mxbai's cosine and the STS-B human scores, by layer:

<PlotlyChart data={scaleData.stsb_pearson.data} layout={scaleData.stsb_pearson.layout} />

| Layer | Pearson r | Interpretation                                      |
| ----- | --------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| L0    | 0.500     | Baseline: shared vocabulary already predictive      |
| L6    | 0.611     | Early layers add some signal                        |
| L12   | 0.507     | **Drops back**, the attractor washes out the signal |
| L18   | 0.667     | Recovery begins                                     |
| L21   | 0.801     | Sharp rise                                          |
| L24   | **0.875** | Peak, the final representation is most predictive   |

At L12 every pair looks similar, so the correlation with human scores falls back to its L0 level. The last 12 layers rebuild the ordering and end at 0.875, with plain mean-pool cosine and no instruction prefix.

## The last layer rewrites most dimensions

For each of the 1,024 dimensions, I found the layer whose update (the change in the mean-pool vector from the layer before) is largest on average over all 2,758 STS-B sentences:

<PlotlyChart data={scaleData.peak_layer_dist.data} layout={scaleData.peak_layer_dist.layout} />

| Layer range           | Dimensions | Fraction  |
| --------------------- | ---------- | --------- |
| L1 to L5 (early)      | 35         | 3.4%      |
| L5 to L20 (middle)    | 27         | 2.6%      |
| L20 to L23 (late-mid) | 68         | 6.6%      |
| **L24 (final)**       | **894**    | **87.3%** |

894 of 1,024 dimensions get their largest single update in the very last layer. [check: earlier drafts said 94.8% of dimensions peak at L24, but 894 / 1,024 is 87.3%. The layer ranges in the table also overlap at L5 and L20.] Earlier layers still build the context that L24 uses, but the model makes a large rewrite right at the output.

Little of layer 0 survives that rewrite. Across the same 2,758 sentences, the correlation between a dimension's value at L0 and at L24 is centred at r = 0.024 (standard deviation 0.087). No dimension reaches |r| > 0.5, and the most preserved one has r = 0.373:

<PlotlyChart data={scaleData.preservation_hist.data} layout={scaleData.preservation_hist.layout} />

My guess for why the last layer does so much is contrastive training. The loss is applied to the output vector, so its gradient reaches the last layer most directly. [check: the old text said the loss "flows back through mean pooling", but mxbai is trained and served with CLS pooling.]

## Key Takeaways

- In mxbai, MiniLM and Qwen3, the vector is close to useless for search until the last quarter of the model. Half the final nDCG@10 first shows up at 75% to 100% depth on 37 of 39 model and dataset pairs. The two misses are Quora on the two encoders.
- The middle layers search worse than layer 0. A plain average of context-free word vectors beats mxbai's layer 12 (18.57 vs 10.34) and Qwen3's layer 12 (16.61 vs 3.95).
- The reason shows up on pairs. At L12, SNLI contradictions reach a mean cosine of 0.914, and the STS-B correlation with human scores drops to 0.507. The last layers bring it back to 0.875.
- 894 of mxbai's 1,024 dimensions get their largest update in the final layer.
