[Token Search](/blog/token-search) made its case on one tokenizer: run BM25 on a model's own BPE token IDs — with two small fixes, un-fragmenting words and stemming-by-expansion — and it matches a Porter-stemmed English analyzer across 12 NanoBEIR datasets, with no stopword list, no stemmer, no per-language config. Fair question: is that a property of _token-native retrieval_, or a lucky property of tiktoken's `o200k` vocabulary?

So I swapped the tokenizer for **BERT's WordPiece** (`bert-base-uncased`) and reran the identical stack. Different vocabulary size (30k vs 200k) and a different scheme — WordPiece marks continuation pieces with `##` instead of BPE's leading spaces — and it even fragments words differently:

```
wordpiece:  playing | footballers | disqualified | un ##sport ##sman ##like
o200k:      playing | football ers  | dis qualified | uns ports man like
```

WordPiece keeps common words whole and only shatters rare ones; `o200k` splits more evenly. So the un-fragmentation fix has _less_ to do on WordPiece — but its single whole-word token for a rare word is too rare to match anything, which is exactly where soft subword coverage earns its keep.

Same recipe, 12 NanoBEIR, NDCG@10:

| system                              |      mean |       p50 |   p25 |
| ----------------------------------- | --------: | --------: | ----: |
| `word_en` (Porter-stemmed analyzer) |     0.545 |     0.591 | 0.264 |
| BPE `o200k` + stack                 |     0.547 |     0.605 | 0.237 |
| WordPiece, raw BM25                 |     0.532 |     0.574 | 0.242 |
| **WordPiece + stack**               | **0.549** | **0.612** | 0.237 |

Two verdicts from the cluster bootstrap:

- **WordPiece + stack ties the stemmed analyzer** — +0.004 vs `word_en`, the CI straddles zero (6 wins / 6 losses). The same tie the BPE version gets.
- **WordPiece is statistically _equivalent_ to BPE** — +0.002 vs `o200k`, and the whole confidence interval `[−0.003, +0.007]` sits inside the equivalence margin. Not merely "no significant difference" — provably the same representation, to within a rounding error.

The base (raw WordPiece BM25, −0.012 under the analyzer) trails, and GroupCov coverage + Porter-expansion close it — doing the same lifting they do on BPE, and landing the best median of any system here (0.612).

So the token-native result isn't a tiktoken artifact. Swap the entire subword scheme and the conclusion is unchanged: **BM25 on a model's own token IDs, with un-fragmentation and stemming-by-expansion, matches a hand-built stemmed analyzer** — whichever tokenizer the model happens to use.

One last reason WordPiece is the right thing to test: it's **[SPLADE](/blog/if-splade)'s vocabulary**. SPLADE learns term weights on exactly `bert-base` WordPiece. So the 0.549 here is the literal BM25 floor SPLADE is built on top of — the "matching a stemmed analyzer is the unweighted ceiling, and learned weights are the only lever past it" argument from Token Search now stands on the exact vocabulary the learned model uses.
