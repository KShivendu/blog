Anthropic's [Jacobian lens](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) reads what a language model holds at each layer. I ported it to an embedding model, [mxbai-embed-large-v1](https://huggingface.co/mixedbread-ai/mxbai-embed-large-v1), to read what each word adds to the sentence vector. On "A man is playing a guitar on stage.", each word reads as itself up to layer 20. In layers 21 to 23, every word folds onto the topic: at layer 23, even `is` reads as **guitarist, guitar, musician**. The result rests on one sentence.

> This is part of the embedding geometry series. [Layer by Layer](/blog/embedding-layers) found that mxbai's sentences only separate in its last layers (separability 0.22 at layer 20, 3.66 at layer 24). [Embedding Models Only Become Searchable in Their Last Layers](/blog/transformer-geometry-at-scale) showed search quality appears at 75% to 100% depth in three different models. Those posts measure when the sentence vector forms. This one reads what goes into it.

## How the lens reads an embedding model

The Jacobian lens was built for language models. It asks which output tokens move if you push on a hidden state. An embedding model has no output tokens. It returns one vector, taken from the CLS token (a special token BERT puts in front of every input). So I measure how a word moves that vector instead:

```
 ① take "guitar" at layer 12 in the probe sentence

   "A man is playing a guitar on stage."
                          │
                          │ h  (its hidden state,
                          │     1,024 numbers)
                          ▼
 ② add 0.01 × h at layer 12 of another sentence

   "The children are running in the park."
                     ▲
                     └── + 0.01 × h
                          │
                          ▼
 ③ run layers 13 to 24 and watch the CLS vector

   Δ = (new CLS − old CLS) / 0.01
                          │
                          ▼
 ④ repeat at 20 spots in 12 sentences, average Δ
                          │
                          ▼
 ⑤ find the 3 closest of 4,000 words (by cosine)

   guitar, guitarist, musician
```

Δ is what "guitar" at layer 12 adds to a sentence vector. The 4,000 words are the first lowercase words in BERT's vocabulary, each embedded by mxbai.

## The lens only works averaged

Steps ② and ④ are the part that makes it work, as the paper also found. If I nudge "guitar" inside its own sentence only, the readout is noise. `guitar` at layer 0 decodes to kitchen, violin, anger, and `man` at layer 12 decodes to build, joy, read.

The angle between the seven words' Δs shows it without any dictionary. Two random directions in 1,024 dimensions sit about 90° apart. Measured in the one sentence, the words are 90° to 95° apart from layer 0 to layer 17, as unrelated as random vectors. Averaged over the 12 other sentences, they are 64° to 75° apart, and the readout makes sense.

At layer 12, the four content words read as themselves:

```
man      man, men, brothers
playing  guitarist, guitar, playing
guitar   guitar, guitarist, musician
stage    stage, broadway, stages
```

- `guitar` and `stage` are right from layer 0. `stage` has stage as its top word at every layer from 0 to 23.
- `playing` starts at layer 0 as pitch, writer, player, the sports sense. By layer 5 it reads musician, guitarist, guitar, because its hidden state has taken in the words around it.
- `a`, `is` and `on` stay noise in the middle. At layer 12, `on` reads breathing, child, institution.

## Layers 21 to 23 fold every word onto the topic

At layer 23, every word reads as the sentence's topic:

```
a        guitar, guitarist, musician
is       guitarist, guitar, musician
on       stage, guitar, guitarist
man      man, guitarist, male
```

The angle between the words falls from 74.5° at layer 0 to 58° at layer 21 and 46° at layer 23. The seven words stop pointing seven ways and start pointing one way.

These are the same layers where mxbai's vector becomes usable for search. In the [per-layer search test](/blog/transformer-geometry-at-scale), its CLS vector finds nothing relevant in the top 10 for 95.2% of NanoBEIR queries at layer 20, and for 9.7% at layer 24.

## Limits

- One probe sentence on one model. One of the 12 context sentences, "The band is performing on a small stage.", is close to it.
- Layer 21 is noisy. Six of seven words lose their meaning there (`guitar` reads agents, montreal, about), while layers 20 and 22 read cleanly. I don't know why yet.
- Layer 24 can't be read. A nudge after the last layer can't reach the CLS vector, so Δ is zero for every word.
- Δ comes from one nudge of size 0.01, which treats the model as linear around that point.

## Key Takeaways

- The Jacobian lens works on an embedding model if you measure how each word moves the sentence vector, and decode that against words embedded by the same model.
- It only works averaged. In one sentence, the words point 90° to 95° apart, as randomly as noise.
- Up to layer 20, each word in mxbai reads as itself. At layer 12, `man` reads man, men, brothers.
- In layers 21 to 23, every word folds onto the topic. At layer 23, `is` reads guitarist, guitar, musician.
